Weierstrass's M-test

Weierstrass’s M-test is a way to garuntee an infinite series of functions converges absolutely and uniformly. The M-test is important, because convergence in one of these modes does not necessarily imply convergence in the other.

Weierstrass M-test

Let (𝑓𝑛(𝑥)) be an infinite sequence of functions. We are going to make a convergence statement for all 𝑥∈𝐸, where 𝐸 is just some set.

Our job is to supply a sequence of non-negative numbers 𝑀𝑛, for each 𝑛, such that |𝑓𝑛(𝑥)|≤𝑀𝑛 for all 𝑛 and 𝑥∈𝐸. So, independent of the value of 𝑥, we need to find a constant upper bound.

If we can show that

∑𝑛=1∞𝑀𝑛<∞

then the series

𝑆(𝑥)=∑𝑛=1∞𝑓𝑛(𝑥)

converges absolutely and uniformly on the set 𝐸. That is the entire result: if we can find a sequence of real numbers that converge and provide a constant upper bound for each 𝑓𝑛(𝑥) on 𝐸, then we get very powerful convergence proprties. These properties are defined next.

Absolute Convergence

The sequence of functions (𝑓𝑛(𝑥)) over 𝐸 remains the same throughout this post. The series 𝑆(𝑥) converges absolutely at a point 𝑥𝑝∈𝐸 if the series of numbers

∑𝑛=1∞|𝑓𝑛(𝑥𝑝)|

converges. This is a point-wise property of functions, found for each 𝑥𝑝∈𝐸. If the series converges for every 𝑥∈𝐸, then the series 𝑆(𝑥) converges absolutely on the set 𝐸.

Uniform Convergence

Absolute convergence implies point-wise convergence, but it says nothing about the rates at which neighboring points converge. Uniform convergence will say something about how the entire function sequence converges.

Let the partial sums be defined as 𝑆𝑁(𝑥)=∑𝑛=1𝑁𝑓𝑛(𝑥). The limit of 𝑆𝑁(𝑥) as 𝑁→∞ for 𝑥∈𝐸 is just the infinite series 𝑆(𝑥).

The sequence of partial sums 𝑆𝑁(𝑥) converges uniformly to 𝑆(𝑥) if for every 𝜀>0 there is an index 𝑁 such that for all 𝑛≥𝑁 (that is, for the tail series),

|𝑆𝑛(𝑥)−𝑆(𝑥)|<𝜀

for all 𝑥∈𝐸. What does this do? Effectively, after a certain 𝑁 point which is independent of 𝑥 (but maybe dependent on 𝜀), we are always within 𝜀 of convergence. The lack of dependence on 𝑥∈𝐸 is critical — it is what allows us to say the entire function over 𝐸 is getting closer to the limit.

Passing Thoughts

The Weierstrass’s M-test seems to me similiar in spirit to Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem. Above, Weierstrass is concerned primarily by the convergence of infinite series of functions. In contrast, Lebesgue’s celebrated theorem is about swapping limits and integrals in measure theory. So they really are different, but the machinery in both seems to be the same.

Both theorems take wild sequences of (𝑓𝑛) and seek a tamer alternative over 𝐸. Lebesgue requires an integrable function 𝑔(𝑥), which needs to be larger than |𝑓𝑛(𝑥)|. Weierstrass requires a numeric sequence 𝑀𝑛, which is greater or equal to |𝑓𝑛(𝑥)|. Lebesgue’s dominating function is independent of 𝑛; Weierstrass’s M is independent of 𝑥; both are simpler than their original sequence, and are greater to or equal to the elements of the sequence in question.
