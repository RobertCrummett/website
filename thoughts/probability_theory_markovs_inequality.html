Probability Theory — Markov's Inequality

Most inequalities in the thoery of probability follow from Markov’s inequality. It is a fundamental starting point for other results, so it pays to know it carefully.

Markov’s Inequality

Let 𝑍 be a random variable, 𝑍≥0 almost surely. For all 𝑡>0,

ℙ(𝑍≥𝑡)≤𝔼𝑍𝑡.

This proof I learned from John Duchi of Stanford. By definition, ℙ(𝑍≥𝑡)=𝔼𝕀{𝑍≥𝑡}. The key here is to notice that 𝑍/𝑡≥𝕀{𝑍≥𝑡} for all 𝑍≥0 and 𝑡>0. Consider the case 𝑍/𝑡≥1 and 𝑍/𝑡<1, and the result follows. Thus 𝔼𝕀{𝑍≥𝑡}≤𝔼(𝑍/𝑡)=𝔼𝑍/𝑡, the result. ∎

What is so powerful about this? What I have observed is that folks leverage this inequality in one chief way: transformations. Given some transform 𝑓 which maps to the positive reals, it follows ℙ(𝑓(𝑍)≥𝑓(𝑡))≤𝔼𝑓(𝑍)/𝑓(𝑡). For instance, observe the next example — it is really nothing more than Markov’s inequality dressed up pretty.

Chebyshev’s Inequality

Let 𝑍∈ℒ︀2 (i.e. Var(𝑍) is finite). Then

ℙ(𝑍≥𝔼𝑍+𝑡or𝑍≤𝔼𝑍−𝑡)≤Var(𝑍)𝑡2.

Whoa! Is that really Markov’s inequality? Sure is. Notice we did not require 𝑍≥0. We use the trick mentioned above:

ℙ(𝑍≥𝔼𝑍+𝑡or𝑍≤𝔼𝑍−𝑡)=ℙ((𝑍−𝔼𝑍)2≥𝑡2).

Now it is clear we are dealing with a non-negative random variable. For 𝑡2>0, we invoke Markov’s ineqality and 𝑍∈ℒ︀2 to see the famous result in a new light

ℙ((𝑍−𝔼𝑍)2≥𝑡2)≤𝔼(𝑍−𝔼𝑍)2𝑡2=Var(𝑍)𝑡2.∎

So we see that Chebyshev’s inequality follows directly from Markov’s inequality.

Chernoff Bounds

The proof involves another application of Markov’s inequality (big suprise). This bound is interesting because it is exponential in 𝑡.

Let 𝑍∈ℒ︀1 an 𝑡≥0. Then

ℙ(𝑍≥𝔼𝑍+𝑡)≤min𝜆≥0𝑀𝑍−𝔼𝑍(𝜆)𝑒−𝜆𝑡,

where 𝑀𝑍−𝔼𝑍(𝜆) is the moment generating function of 𝑍−𝔼𝑍,

𝑀𝑋(𝜆)=𝔼[𝑒−𝜆𝑋],

which can be identified as a linear combination of Laplace transform. I have also heard it called a double sided Laplace transform. Unlike the characteristic function, the moment generating function is not garunteed to exist. The integrand must decay sufficiently fast.

Proof of the above follows by application of 𝑓(𝑥)=𝑒−𝜆𝑥 with 𝜆≥0 and invokation of Markov’s inequality. Since 𝜆 is a free parameter, we are free to minimize it to find the tightest bound possible.
