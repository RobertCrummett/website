Proof of Young's inequality

This proof leverages properties of convex functions and Hölder conjugates. I like the proof because it is small, and reasonable.

Define 𝑓(𝑡)=𝑡𝑞/𝑞, a convex function on the interval [0,∞).

One characterization of convex functions is that they are everywhere above their tangent lines. For our convex function 𝑓(𝑡), this means that for any 𝑏,𝑐≥0,

𝑓(𝑏)≥𝑓(𝑐)+𝑓′(𝑐)(𝑏−𝑐).

Let 𝑝 and 𝑞 be Hölder conjugates (i.e. 1/𝑝+1/𝑞=1 and 𝑝,𝑞>1).

Now let 𝑐=𝑎𝑝−1.

𝑏𝑞𝑞≥𝑎(𝑝−1)𝑞𝑞+𝑎(𝑝−1)(𝑞−1)(𝑏−𝑎𝑝−1)=𝑎𝑝𝑞+𝑎(𝑏−𝑎𝑝−1)=𝑎𝑝𝑞+𝑎𝑏−𝑎𝑝=(1−𝑞)𝑎𝑝𝑞+𝑎𝑏=−𝑎𝑝𝑝+𝑎𝑏.

The inequality follows by rearrangement. The jumps between the lines are made by recognizing Hölder conjugate identities. The jump from the first line to the second line follows after recognizing (𝑝−1)𝑞=𝑝 and (𝑝−1)(𝑞−1)=1. The jump from the second line to the third line follows after seeing (1−𝑞)/𝑞=−1/𝑝.
